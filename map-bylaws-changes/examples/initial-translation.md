# Initial Translation (example)
An abridged, genericized example: sections 1–3 of the plain-language translation of a federated nonprofit's bylaws, made for a real refactor and recast for a fictional organization, the Network, whose members send delegates to an annual Assembly. Names, colors, figures and legal references are illustrative. Each numbered item is one rule from the original bylaws, restated in plain language. Bracketed notes are the reviewer's questions and observations; they are not part of the rules.

## 1. Purpose and Powers
1. The Network is organized for charitable and educational purposes under Section 501(c)(3).
2. It supports its member communities by connecting them with resources and with each other.
3. Its purposes are defined more fully in Article 3(A) of the Articles of Incorporation.
4. The Board adopts the Network's Mission and Values Statement.
    1. Until the first Assembly, only the Board may amend it.
    2. After the first Assembly, only the Assembly may amend it.
    3. The Mission and Values Statement must reflect and state the purposes defined in Article 3(A) of the Articles of Incorporation.
    4. Member organization MOUs must incorporate [Note: Does "incorporate" mean include the full Statement in each MOU, or incorporate it by reference?] the Mission and Values Statement.
5. The Mission and Values Statement guides decisions by the Board, Officers, and members, but does not change any rights, responsibilities, or procedures established by these Bylaws or the Articles of Incorporation.
6. In any conflict, the Articles of Incorporation control over these Bylaws, and these Bylaws control over the Mission and Values Statement.

## 2. Official Seal, Emblem, and Office
1. The Network's official seal is a circle divided into two halves.
2. The left half is Signal Red (#C0392B).
3. The right half is Harbor Blue (#1F6FB2), divided into six connected points.
4. The center is white (#FFFFFF).
5. The Network's official emblem combines the seal with the organization's name in a condensed bold typeface.
6. The dots over the letters "i" in the name are connected in the same style as the seal. [Note: I think we should remove the seal and emblem specifications from the Bylaws and place them in separate brand standards documentation. State law gives nonprofits the power to have a corporate seal but does not require the bylaws to specify it. We shouldn't have to amend the bylaws to change the hex value of a logo.]
7. The Board selects the Network's business office, which is called the National Office. [Note: We need to clarify whether "National Office" means the corporation's principal office, registered office, or a separate operational office.]

## 3. Fiscal and Legal
### 3.1 Fiscal Year
1. The Network's fiscal year runs from October 1 through September 30.

### 3.2 Fidelity Bonds
[Note: Does the Network currently carry fidelity-bond coverage? Is it actually required by law or by some other external requirement (grant, contract, insurer, etc.)? If not, I think we should consider moving this from the Bylaws to a separate financial policy. UPDATE: A search of the shared drive found no evidence that we carry the fidelity bonds the bylaws require.]

1. The Network must maintain fidelity bonds covering Board-appointed officers, employees, and agents who handle the Network's funds, property, or other assets.
2. The Board approves the form and amount of this coverage.
3. The bonds must protect the Network against losses caused by fraud, theft, misappropriation, or other dishonest acts.
4. Any money or property recovered after a loss must be returned to the Network entity or entities [Note: What "entity or entities" does this refer to? As far as I understand, the Network is a single corporation. Is this leftover language from some other organizational structure?] that suffered the loss.
5. If more than one Network entity suffered the loss and the Network does not recover the full amount, the recovery must be divided among them in proportion to their losses.

### 3.3 Bank Accounts and Depositories
[Note: Do we need to specify this much about bank administration in the Bylaws? It may be cleaner for the Bylaws to give the Board authority over the Network's finances and let the Board operate the details through policy or delegation.]

1. The Board selects the financial institutions that hold the Network's funds, property, and other assets.
2. The Board designates, by title, who is responsible for controlling those assets.

### 3.4 Checking, Savings, and Depository Accounts
1. All cash received by the Network must be deposited into the designated General Fund [Note: Do we have something formally called the "General Fund," or is this legacy language? UPDATE: A search of the shared drive found no evidence of a General Fund as the bylaws describe it.] checking account.
2. Money may be withdrawn from that account only by check or letter of transmittal signed by any two Officers [Note: How is this supposed to work with modern electronic banking, ACH, cards, online transfers, etc.? This sounds like it was written around an older banking model.] (the President, Vice President, Secretary, or Treasurer) or their designees. [Note: This seems to mean each of those Officers can designate someone to act in their place. But how does someone actually become a designee? Does it need to be in writing, approved by anyone else, or recorded somewhere? This feels underspecified. I also found no evidence that we have a Vice President.]
3. The Board must approve any transfer of other Network assets or property [Note: What does this mean in practice? What assets are being transferred between financial institutions other than money in the Network's accounts?] between financial institutions.

### 3.5 Budget
[Note: This seems like too many layers for the Network's actual capacity. Do we even have a functioning Finance Committee? The real decision points seem much simpler: someone proposes a budget, the Board decides whether to send it forward, and the Assembly gives final approval. The Finance Committee "may review" line especially doesn't create any actual decision-making authority; anyone can give feedback. This could collapse to something like "The Treasurer proposes a budget; the Board approves it for submission; the Assembly gives final approval," and even defining who has to propose the budget may be unnecessary machinery.]

1. The Treasurer prepares the Network's budget.
2. The Finance Committee may review the proposed budget.
3. The Treasurer submits the proposed budget to the Board for review and revision.
4. The Board recommends a budget to the Assembly.
5. Once the Assembly has been established, the Treasurer must provide the budget to the Assembly at least 15 days before the annual Assembly. [Note: This makes the budget process depend on the annual Assembly happening on schedule. Given that this year's Assembly was delayed, we should consider whether those two processes need to be coupled this tightly.]
6. The Assembly approves the budget at the annual meeting.
7. Any delegate may propose an amendment to the budget.
    1. If another delegate seconds the proposal, the Assembly may vote on it. [Note: I don't think we need to hardcode the motion-and-second procedure specifically for budget amendments. The Assembly's general meeting rules should handle this.]

### 3.6 Contracts
1. The Network's Counsel must review every contract and approve its legal form before the Network signs it. [Note: I read the original "approved as to form" as meaning legal and form review, not that Counsel decides whether the Network should enter the contract. Is that correct?]
2. Contracts valued at $2,500 or less may be signed by staff authorized by both the President and Secretary. [Note: How is this authorization actually created and recorded? Does it need to be in writing? Is it authorization for a particular contract, or can someone be given standing authority to sign contracts within defined limits? We should make clear what counts as authorization so there is an actual record of who has signing authority.]
3. Contracts valued above $2,500 must be signed by both the President and Secretary.

### 3.7 Conflict of Interest
[Note: There are additional conflict-of-interest rules later in the Officers section, including disclosure and recusal requirements for Board members. The core conflict rules should probably be consolidated in one place, with other sections referring back to them where needed, rather than splitting related rules across the Bylaws.]

1. The Board must maintain a conflict of interest policy.
2. Officers, Directors, key employees, and anyone else designated by the Board must follow that policy.

### 3.8 Compensation
1. The President, Vice President, and Treasurer must approve compensation for all paid Network personnel.
2. The Board must approve compensation for any Board-appointed officer.
3. Compensation approved for a Board-appointed officer must be published on the Network's website.
